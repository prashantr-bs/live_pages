Does what we capture match what VoiceOver says?

iPad Air 11" (M2), iPadOS 26.5 · Angular Rule Lab · VoiceOver output captured for every element of all 41 rule pages with the iOS accessibility audit service (pymobiledevice3 developer accessibility list-items, the same engine Accessibility Inspector uses for its VoiceOver "spoken description"; VoiceOver itself cannot be toggled remotely) · our capture = the read-only in-process DOM layer (v5) scans · plus a 44-min screen recording of real VoiceOver navigating every rule page (watch the recording). Its on-screen caption panel was OCR'd at 2 fps; the full announcement after each pause is kept. · 1 Oct 2026

Key findings

  • Names are right. Against real VoiceOver (screen recording), the accessible name we capture agrees for 97% of 230 matched elements. Identical wording, ignoring hints such as "Double-tap to…" and landmarks such as "end, main", is 72% for our capture and 68% for the audit service.
  • The audit service is not a perfect proxy for VoiceOver's speech. Its roles differ from what VoiceOver says: it drops "Text field" and "Tab", and turns checkboxes and switches into "0/1, Button, Toggle". Real VoiceOver says "Checkbox, ticked", "Switch button, on" and "Radio button, ticked, 1 of 2". Our capture is closer to real speech than the audit service is.
  • What our caption still lacks: the state word (VoiceOver says ticked / unticked, on / off, Pressed, Selected after the role, whereas we prefix "Selected"), the heading level ("Heading level 2"), the list position ("1 of 2"), and a disabled word for disabled controls (audit: "Not Enabled"; not exercised in the recording). "Tab" should not carry an extra "Button".
  • Images: VoiceOver often replaces "Image" with its own description ("Profile" then "Chart, Person"), and an SVG used as an <img> is read by its inner text ("50% OFF") rather than its alt.
  • Verdicts: the label rules have 0 false positives on the test cases. Recall is limited by AI-pending checks and iOS rule semantics, not by wrong names.
